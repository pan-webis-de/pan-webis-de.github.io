Keynotes

Bringing Computer Science Tools to Social Science: The Methods Hub
Johannes Kiesel
GESIS

In computer science in general and at PAN in particular researchers develop tools that can be highly useful for social science research, but social scientists often struggle to apply them to their research questions. While tools may be technically reusable, their documentation rarely explains their different use cases, assumptions, or practical application beyond the original context. Moreover, finding the right tool for a task is a challenge from scientists outside of the original field.

The Methods Hub bridges this gap by making computational methods discoverable, accessible, and practically reusable from a social science perspective. It organizes methods around research use cases and provides practical information, tutorials, and interactive online environments to help social scientists apply them to their own tasks in a reproducible manner.

Johannes Kiesel is head of team BDA at GESIS.

Participation Modalities

To participate at PAN, first register for your task of choice at CLEF, then follow the instructions below.

Submission

We use TIRA for all submissions to PAN. Please go to tira.io, create an account, and register for the individual tasks you want to participate in. You need to submit your software or your results via TIRA. You can find all submission guides in TIRA's forum.

Data

You can download PAN's datasets here. For details, please check the individual task's website.

Evaluation and Baseline Code

All code used at PAN is published at GitHub. You can find all validators, evaluators, and baselines there.

Software Submissions

PAN promotes reproducible science with software submissions. Please prepare and submit your software as Docker image(s). You can find guides and examples in the resources linked above. Some tasks allow only software submissions and only release the test data after the conference.

Paper Submission and Presentation

PAN is co-located with CLEF 2026 in Jena, Germany. Every participant is expected to write a notebook paper describing their approach to CLEF (published at CEUR-WS, which is indexed by DBLP). At the CLEF conference, all submissions will be presented as talks or posters. CLEF will be a hybrid conference.
